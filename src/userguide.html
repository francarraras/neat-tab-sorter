User Guide

Click the Neat Tab Sorter icon, or press , to sort your tabs. Only the window you're in is sorted, and tabs are only ever moved: nothing is closed, reloaded, ungrouped or unpinned.

Changed your mind? Right-click the icon and choose Undo last sort. Each window remembers the order it had before its last sort until you close it or restart your browser. You can also give undo a keyboard shortcut (currently ) on the shortcuts page.

The icon's right-click menu also lets you sort a different way: picking a sort order sorts the window right away and makes that order the default. If the icon isn't in your toolbar, click the puzzle-piece Extensions button and pin Neat Tab Sorter.

Tab groups

Chrome's tab groups are always kept intact and placed to the left of all ungrouped tabs. By default, groups are put in alphabetical order by name and the tabs inside them are sorted with the same settings as ungrouped tabs.

  • To choose the order of your groups, number them: "1. Work", "2. Shopping", "10. Later". Numbers sort naturally, so 10 comes after 2. Groups without a name go after the named ones.
  • Turn off Sort tab groups by name to keep your groups in their current order.
  • Turn off Sort tabs inside tab groups to leave the tabs in your groups exactly where they are and only sort your ungrouped tabs.

Split view

Chrome ends a split view when one of its tabs is moved away from the other, so tabs in a split view are never moved. Instead, the other tabs are sorted around them.

Settings

Sort tabs by

  • Title: alphabetical by page title. Unread counters at the start of a title, like "(3) Inbox", are ignored, so tabs don't move around as the count changes.
  • URL: alphabetical by address, ignoring the protocol and a leading "www.", so www.example.com sorts together with example.com.
  • Custom (group by site): keeps tabs from the same site together without putting the sites in alphabetical order. See Custom sort below.
  • Recently used: the tabs you used most recently come first.

Every sort order compares numbers naturally ("Episode 2" comes before "Episode 10"), ignores upper and lower case, and leaves tabs that tie where they are, relative to each other.

Sort pinned tabs

Pinned tabs are left alone by default. If you turn this on, pinned tabs stay together at the start of the window and are sorted the same way as the rest of your tabs.

Group suspended tabs together

Tab suspenders based on The Great Suspender, like The Marvellous Suspender, change the address of each tab they suspend and keep the original address inside it:

chrome-extension://noogafoofpebimajpfpamcfhoaifemoa/suspended.html#ttl=Google&pos=0&uri=https://www.google.com/

By default, Neat Tab Sorter sorts suspended tabs by their original address, as if they weren't suspended. If you turn this on, suspended tabs are moved to the left (of each tab group, and of your ungrouped tabs) and sorted among themselves.

Suspended tabs are recognized automatically for any extension that uses this format. Chrome's built-in Memory Saver doesn't change addresses, so tabs it puts to sleep are always sorted normally.

Custom sort

Custom sort gathers the tabs from each site (for example, every github.com tab) into a block, but instead of putting the blocks in alphabetical order, it keeps them in the order the sites already appear in. To choose the order of your sites, drag a few tabs into place, then sort.

  • Group from: with Left to right, sites are ordered by their leftmost tab. With Right to left, they're ordered by their rightmost tab instead, so a site you've just opened a new tab for moves to the end.
  • Preserve order within groups: tabs from the same site keep their current order (the default). If you turn it off, they're sorted by URL.

Tips

  • Want to experiment? Undo puts everything back, or you can open a few tabs in a new window and try different settings there.
  • Settings are saved as soon as you change them and are synced with your browser profile, so they apply in every window.

Privacy

Neat Tab Sorter doesn't collect or send any data. It reads your tabs' titles and addresses only to sort them, on your computer. Your settings are saved with your browser profile, and each window's undo order is forgotten when you close it or restart your browser.
